# Fact-check record

Date of this check: **21 October 2026**. Method: the claims shown on the website were compared with official or
primary sources using web search and by reading the UN SDG 7 page. This file says exactly how strong each check
was, so nothing is over-claimed.

**Verification levels**
- **A — read:** the official page was opened and the figure read from it.
- **B — search:** an official/primary source was found through search and its figure quoted in the result; the
  full report was not read page by page.
- **C — general knowledge:** well-known, widely documented fact; **not** re-verified in this pass.

## Statistics on the Home page

| Shown on the site | Verdict | Source and notes | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~655 million people without electricity (2024) | ✅ matches | UN SDG 7 page: "about 655 million people lacked electricity in 2024". The previous report year was 666 million (2023). | A |
| ~2.1 billion without clean cooking fuels (2023) | ✅ matches | *Tracking SDG 7: The Energy Progress Report 2025* (IRENA, IEA, UN, World Bank, WHO): 2.1 billion in 2023. The UN page says "over two billion" for 2024. | A / B |
| ~30% of global electricity from renewables (2024) | ✅ reasonable | Ember *Global Electricity Review 2025*: hydro 14.3% + wind 8.1% + solar 6.9% ≈ 29%, before bioenergy and others. | Target year 2030 | ✅ | UN SDG 7 targets all say "By 2030". | A |

## Facts and tips

| Claim | Verdict | Source and notes | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| LED bulbs use **at least 75%** less energy than incandescent bulbs and last up to 25× longer | ✅ **corrected** (was "up to 90%") | US Department of Energy, *LED Lighting*: residential LEDs, especially ENERGY STAR models, use at least 75% less energy and last up to 25 times longer. | B |
| Solar panel costs fell by more than 80% since 2010; solar is among the cheapest new electricity | ✅ conservative | IRENA *Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024*: utility-scale solar PV electricity cost $0.043/kWh and about 90% lower than in 2010 (the 90% figure seen via secondary coverage of the report); 91% of new renewable capacity was cheaper than the cheapest new fossil option. | B |
| Hydropower is the largest source of renewable electricity | ✅ | Ember 2025: hydro 14.3% of global electricity, the largest low-carbon source. | B |
| Denmark gets a large share of electricity from wind, more than almost any other country | ✅ | About 58–59% of Danish generation in 2024 (World Nuclear Association: 20.6 of 35.3 TWh). The comparison with "almost any other country" was not checked against a ranking. | B |
| ~655 million people lack electricity (technology fact) | ✅ **corrected** (was "700 million", contradicting the Home page) | Same as above. | A |
| Washing in cold water saves most of a washer's energy ("up to 90%") | ⚠️ plausible | Widely repeated with ENERGY STAR attribution that ~90% of a (conventional) washer's energy heats the water; found through secondary sources, not the ENERGY STAR page itself. | B (weaker) |
| Under-inflated tires cost up to 3% fuel economy | ✅ | fueleconomy.gov, as quoted in fact-check coverage ("up to 3%"). | B |
| Standby power is 5–10% of home electricity | ✅ | US DOE/OSTI paper (5–10% of US residential electricity); EU estimates 5–10%; IEA material cites about 10% for OECD homes. | B |

## Not re-verified in this pass (level C)

Historical and general statements: Becquerel's photovoltaic effect (1839), the first electricity-generating wind
turbine by James Blyth (1887), the first practical silicon solar cell at Bell Labs (1954), oil crises of the 1970s,
the UN founded in 1945 with 193 members, the SDGs and the Paris Agreement (2015); panel lifetime of 25–30 years;
panel efficiency above 22%; pumped-storage hydro, green hydrogen, smart-grid and floating-solar statements; the
statement that fossil-fuel air pollution contributes to millions of premature deaths a year.

The calculator uses **0.45 kg CO₂/kWh** and **21 kg CO₂ absorbed per tree per year**. Both are simplified
averages that the app labels as estimates; they were not re-verified here (real values vary by country and tree).

## Corrections made as a result

1. LED claim changed from "up to 90%" to "at least 75%" (and "up to 25× longer") in the fact and the tip.
2. Home statistics updated to the latest official figures and now show their year.
3. The technology fact (700 million) now agrees with the Home page (~655 million).
4. "Share of renewables" relabelled as **electricity** (the number is the electricity share).
5. Targets are labelled as shown in simplified wording.
